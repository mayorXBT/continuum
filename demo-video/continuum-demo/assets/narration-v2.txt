I kept watching how traders actually move money on-chain, and I ran into the same thing every time. The compliant apps they use check your ID once, at the door, and then never look again.

That leaves you two bad choices. Lock your money up to earn, and lose access to it. Or keep it liquid, and risk that the day your status changes, the app freezes your funds and keeps them.

So the people with real money to put in don't. They stay out.

This is Continuum. Staking where the rule lives inside the token, so it gets checked every time the money moves.

You put in MON, and get stMON back. That's your staked money in liquid form. It earns while you hold it, and you can use it right away. Nothing is locked.

Every time you send it, the token asks Cleanverse whether the person is allowed, before it moves.

Here's the moment that proves it. Both of these wallets hold a valid ID. But one sits below the trust level our pool requires, so the token refuses to move to it. That rule lives on Cleanverse's side, not ours. We couldn't wave them through even if we wanted to.

And if your status is revoked, your money isn't stolen. You ask to cash out to an approved wallet, a reviewer checks it, and your money comes back. Everything you put in, plus what it earned.

That's the whole point. Most designs freeze and confiscate. Continuum freezes and refunds.
